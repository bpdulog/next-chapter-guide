02 / Make the meeting useful

Questions to ask HR

It’s okay to take notes, ask for clarification, and follow up by email. You don’t need to make every decision in the room.

01

Pay & severance

  • What is my official last day, and when will my final paycheck arrive?
  • How are unused vacation, earned commissions, bonus, and expenses handled?
  • Is severance offered? What is the amount, schedule, and eligibility basis?
  • What does the agreement ask me to give up, and can I take time to review it?
02

Health & other benefits

  • On what date does my medical, dental, and vision coverage end?
  • When and how will I receive COBRA information and the cost?
  • Can you provide the plan details and contact for the benefits administrator?
  • What happens to life insurance, disability coverage, and HSA/FSA funds?
03

Retirement & equity

  • Who is the 401(k) or pension plan contact, and where are plan documents?
  • Can I change my 401(k) contribution rate for remaining paychecks? What is the cutoff, and does final pay qualify?
  • How does the employer match work on my remaining pay, and when will contributions post and vest?
  • What happens to stock options, RSUs, or other equity, and what dates apply?
  • Where can I find the written rules for my plan or award?
04

Records & next steps

  • Can I have a copy of the separation terms and benefits summary?
  • How can employers verify my role and dates, and may I list a reference?
  • Will you provide information needed for an unemployment claim?
  • Who should I contact if I find a pay or benefits issue later?
✎

Tip: After the meeting, send a short email summarizing what you understood and asking HR to correct anything you missed.

03 / Decisions, in plain language

Know what to compare

Gather the written terms first. Your options depend on your plan, location, and circumstances.

$Severance and final payUnderstand the offer and what is owed+

Ask for the payment amount, dates, tax withholding, benefits continuation, and any conditions. Federal law does not generally require severance, but an agreement, policy, or other law may matter. Federal law does not require an immediate final paycheck; state rules may set different timing. Check your state labor department if timing or amounts are unclear.

DOL: Severance pay ↗DOL: Last paycheck ↗Find your state labor office ↗
+Health coverageCompare COBRA, Marketplace, and other plans+

Confirm your coverage end date and compare premiums, deductibles, doctors, prescriptions, and start dates. COBRA may let eligible people continue the same plan, often at the full premium plus an administrative fee. Marketplace coverage may be available after losing job-based coverage. Election windows and effective dates matter, so check notices promptly and confirm details with the plan.

DOL: COBRA worker FAQ ↗HealthCare.gov: Losing job coverage ↗
◎UnemploymentStart with your state agency+

Eligibility, benefit amounts, waiting periods, and claim requirements vary by state and your situation. Apply through the state agency where you worked, follow its weekly or ongoing requirements, and answer questions accurately. If you receive severance, ask the state agency how to report it; don’t assume how it affects a claim.

DOL: Find your state unemployment office ↗
↗Retirement and equityPause before cashing anything out+

Ask about vesting, fees, plan distribution options, and deadlines for stock awards. Retirement plan choices can include leaving funds in the plan, a direct rollover, or a distribution, depending on plan rules. Taking money out may create taxes and possibly an additional tax. Compare carefully and consider qualified tax or financial advice for your situation.

IRS: Leaving a job and retirement plans ↗IRS: Rollovers ↗
₵401(k) contributions before your last dayWeigh the match against cash you may need soon+

Check your current contribution rate, remaining eligible paychecks, the deadline to change payroll elections, and whether your plan counts any final pay for contributions or matching. Compare the value and vesting of any employer match with the cash you need for bills and health coverage. If you join another employer’s plan this year, track your employee contributions across plans against the annual limit; plan rules may also set lower limits.

IRS: 401(k) contribution limits ↗IRS: Match vesting ↗
◎Student loansFind the right options before payments become difficult+

List each loan, servicer, next due date, and whether it is federal or private. For federal loans, ask your servicer about current income-driven repayment options if your income has dropped; compare any temporary deferment or forbearance with its interest costs and possible effect on forgiveness progress. For private loans, ask the lender or servicer about hardship or lower-payment options and get the terms in writing. Keep making required payments until a change is confirmed.

Federal Student Aid: Repayment questions ↗Federal Student Aid: Temporary relief ↗CFPB: Private loan options ↗
▦Cash flow, bills, and taxesMake a short-term plan from actual dates and amounts+

Write down cash on hand, expected pay and severance dates, unemployment income if approved, and essential bills by due date. Prioritize housing, utilities, food, insurance, and other obligations based on the consequences of missing each payment. Check automatic payments to avoid overdrafts and contact lenders or billers early about hardship options. Severance and unemployment benefits are generally taxable; review withholding or estimated payments so tax time does not bring a surprise.

CFPB: Money after job loss ↗IRS: Tax impact of job loss ↗
▤Notice and workplace rightsAsk what applies to your situation+

Some large layoffs or plant closings may fall under federal or state notice laws, but coverage depends on employer and event details. Ask HR for written information about the decision and any notice. If you believe a rule was not followed, contact the appropriate labor agency or an employment attorney.

04 / Words when they’re hard to find

Borrow a script

Adapt these to your voice. Each button copies the text so you can edit it before sending.

TO HR

Request the written details

Hi [Name], thanks for meeting with me. Could you please send me the written separation terms, benefits end dates, and the best contact for questions about final pay and benefits? I’d also appreciate confirmation of my last day and when I should expect the next documents. Thank you, [Name]
TO HR

Ask for time to review

Hi [Name], I’ve received the proposed agreement. I’d like time to read it carefully and understand the terms. Please confirm the review period and the date by which a response is requested. I’ll follow up with any questions. Thank you, [Name]
